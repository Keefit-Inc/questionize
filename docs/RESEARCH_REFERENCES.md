# Attributed method references

Method references and official answer-key datasets serve different purposes. A logic textbook can motivate a reasoning or writing procedure; it is not an answer label for a GMAT question. AI-authored summaries of a textbook are derivatives, not expert certification.

| Work | Reviewed application | Capability / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Matthew Knachel, *Fundamental Methods of Logic* (2017 original), [publisher](https://wisconsin.pressbooks.pub/logicfundamentals/) | Selected passages on argument analysis, relevance, causal reasoning and uncertainty. Our own abstract procedure adaptation; source examples are not reproduced. | C2/C3 method candidates; C5 candidate scaffold in `ai/questionize/cr_design.py`, tested under E2 v2. |
| P. D. Magnus, Tim Button, Robert Trueman, Richard Zach, with contributions by J. Robert Loftis and Aaron Thomas-Bolduc, *forall x: Calgary*, Fall 2025, [publisher and credits](https://forallx.openlogicproject.org/) | Selected sections on validity, counterexamples and conditionals. Main text and selected-solutions booklet share source lineage, so they are not counted as two independent sources. | C2/C3/C5 method references in the private local candidate context; no measured gain attributed to them yet. |

Both cited works identify [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) on their publisher pages. We retain attribution and the notice of our adaptation; the authors do not endorse this system. The revised 2022 Knachel edition has different terms and is not the edition used here. The local catalog records exact downloaded hashes and partial-review scope. Full PDF acquisition of the Knachel original was unavailable; selected primary web passages were reviewed and archived instead.

The two formal-logic PDFs are reference materials, not hundreds of new GMAT questions. Proprietary OG/website-bank items, publisher explanations and source-specific corrections remain in private local storage. Public experiment records contain aggregate evidence only.
